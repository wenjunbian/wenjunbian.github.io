About me

I am a PhD student in Organizational Behavior at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University.

My research asks how experiences within and beyond work shape well-being, engagement, and leadership. My work spans recovery activities and work engagement, parenting and leadership development, social perceptions of leaders, career growth, and responses to stress. I approach these questions through evidence synthesis, field research, and quantitative analysis.

Beyond my own research, I founded BWJ OB Pluto, an academic column that shares organizational behavior research and reflections on academic life. I value making research accessible and building a community in which researchers can learn from one another.

Research interests

How do activities outside work relate to recovery and engagement at work? My ongoing work uses theoretical synthesis and meta-analysis to examine these connections. Related working paper

How do family experiences and parenting practices relate to leadership? My published work examines benevolent leadership and adolescent leader emergence. Parenting and leadership

How do perceptions of leaders and leader–employee relationships shape leadership emergence and career growth? This line connects work on perceived cuteness, career calling, and career crafting. Career calling study

I am interested in bringing together systematic reviews, meta-analysis, longitudinal data, and computational approaches to understand psychological and organizational processes. Machine-learning study
